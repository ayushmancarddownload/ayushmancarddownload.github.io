Most laptop shopping guides lead with specs that sound impressive but rarely matter for everyday use. Here's what to actually check.

## Match the processor to your workload

For browsing, documents, and video calls, a mid-range processor from the last two generations is plenty. Reserve the top-tier chips for video editing, 3D rendering, or heavy software development.

## RAM matters more than most people think

8GB is workable for basic tasks but tight if you keep many browser tabs open. 16GB is a safer default for most people in 2026, and it's often cheaper to buy it upfront than to upgrade later, since many laptops now solder memory to the board.

## Battery life claims are optimistic

Manufacturer battery estimates are measured under ideal, low-brightness conditions. Expect real-world use to fall 20-30% short of the advertised number, and read independent reviews for a realistic figure.

## Build quality outlasts spec sheets

A laptop with a sturdy hinge and a solid keyboard will often serve you longer than one with slightly faster components in a flimsier body. If you can, test the keyboard and trackpad in person before buying.

The right laptop is the one that matches how you actually work, not the one with the longest spec sheet.
